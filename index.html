Annualize partial-year data. For interim periods, the benchmark figures should reflect the full year (e.g., 6-month revenue of $9M annualizes to $18M). For new entities with a short first period, materiality applies to the period as presented — do not gross-up.

§ 05

Clearly Trivial Threshold

Below this amount, misstatements need not be accumulated. Should be inconsequential by any measure — size, nature, or circumstance.
AS 2810.A4 / AU-C 450
Clearly Trivial as % of Overall Materiality 5%
1% 3% · Conservative 5% · Big 4 Default 10% · Maximum
Source · AFM Survey + Big 4 Methodology In 93% of statutory audits surveyed, the threshold for clearly trivial misstatements was set at 5% of overall materiality. The methodologies of the Big Four often specify a standard percentage of 5%. A misstatement is only clearly trivial if it is inconsequential individually and when aggregated with every other accumulated error. Doubt → accumulate it.
§ 06

Specific Materiality

For particular accounts, transactions, or disclosures where users would react to a smaller misstatement than the overall threshold suggests.
AU-C 320.10(b)
Common candidates: related-party transactions, executive compensation, segment disclosures, regulatory capital, going-concern footnote, debt covenants.
Source · AU-C 320 .10(b) If, in the specific circumstances of the entity, there is one or more particular classes of transactions, account balances, or disclosures for which misstatements of lesser amounts than materiality for the financial statements as a whole could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users … the auditor shall also determine the materiality level or levels to be applied to those particular classes of transactions, account balances, or disclosures.

§ 08

Component Materiality

For consolidated audits. Allocate component-level materiality so that aggregated undetected misstatements remain below group materiality.
AU-C 600 / SAS 149
Group framework (effective for periods ending on or after December 15, 2026 under SAS 149). The group engagement team determines (a) materiality for the group statements as a whole, (b) component materiality (less than group), (c) component performance materiality (to address aggregation risk), and (d) a reporting threshold (≤ group trivial). The same engagement team can be the group auditor and component auditor — the standards still apply.
Source · Journal of Accountancy / Glover et al. Aggregate component materiality can be expressed as a multiple of group overall materiality … applying the benchmark multiple of 2.5 to group overall materiality of $5 million yields a maximum aggregate component materiality of $12.5 million — a reasonable upper bound available for allocation.

Standards Reference Library

Every materiality judgment must be traceable to authoritative literature. These are the originals — not summaries.
PCAOB AS 2105
Consideration of Materiality in Planning and Performing an Audit — The primary US public-company standard. Effective for fiscal years beginning on or after Dec. 15, 2010.
PCAOB AS 2810
Evaluating Audit Results — Governs accumulation of misstatements and the "clearly trivial" threshold.
AICPA AU-C 320
Materiality in Planning and Performing an Audit (PDF) — Non-issuer audits under US GAAS.
AICPA AU-C 450
Evaluation of Misstatements Identified During the Audit (PDF) — AICPA equivalent of AS 2810.
AICPA AU-C 600 / SAS 149
Special Considerations — Audits of Group Financial Statements (PDF) — Component and group materiality. SAS 149 is effective for periods ending on or after Dec 15, 2026.
SEC SAB No. 99
Materiality (Topic 1.M) — The qualitative factors. The 5% rule is a starting point, not a defense.
SEC SAB No. 108
Quantifying Misstatements (Topic 1.N) — Iron-curtain vs. rollover methods for prior-period misstatements.
SEC Munter Statement
Assessing Materiality: Focusing on the Reasonable Investor (Mar 9, 2022) — Quantitatively significant errors cannot generally be made immaterial by qualitative factors.
Supreme Court
TSC Industries v. Northway, 426 U.S. 438 (1976) — The originating "reasonable investor / total mix" test. Reaffirmed in Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 (1988).
FASB
Statement of Financial Accounting Concepts No. 8 (Chapter 3) — FASB's 2018 definition of materiality, aligned with Supreme Court formulation.
A Note on Hierarchy For issuers (SEC registrants) audited under PCAOB standards, AS 2105 governs and SAB 99 / 108 apply to materiality assessments in SEC filings. For non-issuers under AICPA GAAS, AU-C 320, 450, and 600 govern. Both frameworks anchor in the same Supreme Court "reasonable investor" standard. SEC interpretive bulletins do not bind non-issuer audits, but their qualitative factors represent best practice for any materiality judgment.
